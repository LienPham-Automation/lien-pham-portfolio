If your practice offers cosmetic treatments

You have invested in a service
you cannot advertise publicly.

Since September 2025, AHPRA's advertising rules have tightened significantly. For a registered practitioner, penalties now reach $60,000 per breach. Prescription-only treatments cannot be named, priced, discounted, or promoted in any public channel. No paid social naming the treatment, no search ads, no before-and-afters, no influencer content, no promotional offers. The rules apply to your website, your Google profile, your Instagram, your reception signage, and anything a marketing agency publishes on your behalf.

The clinics ignoring those rules are filling chairs. You are watching it happen and your hands are tied.

What you still have is your patient list. People who already know you, already trust you, and are already permitted to hear from you about the care available at your practice. Worked carefully and compliantly, it is the one channel that remains fully open.

Why the patient list works

Existing patients have already consented to hear from your practice about available care. A carefully worded message that introduces a new service offering, without naming or pricing a prescription product, sits outside the public advertising prohibition.

Most practices are not using this channel at all, or sending a single annual recall that nobody reads. A proper sequence, segmented by how long each patient has been away, converts at two to three times the rate of a single message.

Every sequence I write is built to the current AHPRA guidelines from the ground up. Nothing goes out that you would be uncomfortable defending to a regulator.
